Portfolio overview

Artificial intelligence is increasingly influencing financial management through data analysis, forecasting, automation and decision support. This portfolio considers three areas: using Microsoft Power BI to understand business financial performance, learning from the Apple Card credit-limit controversy, and considering the opportunities and risks of generative AI in finance.

The central argument is that digital tools can improve the speed and quality of financial work, but their value depends on reliable data, appropriate human oversight, transparency and responsible governance.

Entry 1 — Using Microsoft Power BI for financial management

Business problem and tool selection

A retailer may hold sales, expense and cash-flow information in separate spreadsheets. This makes it difficult for managers to see the overall financial position, identify changes quickly and make informed decisions. Microsoft Power BI is a business intelligence and data-visualisation tool that can bring information together in interactive reports and dashboards.

Application to a business scenario

For a retail business, a Power BI dashboard could display monthly revenue, operating expenses, profit, cash flow and product performance. Managers could compare periods, identify rising costs and investigate products with lower profit margins. Where suitable forecasting features and reliable historical data are available, the business could also use forecasts as one input into budgeting and planning.

Critical evaluation

Power BI can reduce manual reporting and make financial information easier to understand. It may help managers notice patterns and ask better questions. However, a dashboard does not automatically explain why performance has changed. Poor-quality or incomplete data can produce misleading results, and employees need training to interpret the information. Managers should consider market conditions and operational context before acting on a forecast.

Conclusion

Power BI can strengthen financial visibility and support evidence-based decisions, but it should complement rather than replace professional judgement.

Entry 2 — Algorithmic fairness: the Apple Card controversy

Case background

In 2019, public complaints raised concerns that women and men may have received very different credit limits through the Apple Card programme, which was issued by Goldman Sachs. The complaints prompted questions about how credit decisions were made and whether applicants could understand the reasons for their outcomes.

Investigation and business implications

The New York State Department of Financial Services investigated the allegations. Its 2021 report did not find evidence of unlawful discrimination. This distinction is important: the controversy should be described as allegations and a regulatory investigation, not as proven unlawful gender discrimination.

Critical and ethical evaluation

The case demonstrates why automated or algorithm-supported financial decisions need transparency, clear communication and accountability. Even where unlawful discrimination is not established, customers may lose trust if they cannot understand an important financial decision or challenge an outcome. Financial organisations should test decision systems for unfair patterns, document how decisions are made, provide accessible explanations and maintain effective complaint and review processes.

Visual artefact suggestion: Create a four-step timeline: public concerns (2019) → regulatory investigation → findings published (2021) → lessons on transparency, fairness and accountability.

Conclusion

Responsible AI in financial services requires more than technical accuracy. Fairness, explainability, customer trust and regulatory accountability are also essential.

Entry 3 — Generative AI in financial management

Emerging opportunity

Generative AI may support finance teams with tasks such as summarising financial reports, explaining budget variances, organising information and preparing first drafts of management commentary. For example, a finance manager could use an approved AI system to turn non-confidential monthly figures into a draft summary for review.

Potential business value

These applications may reduce time spent on repetitive writing and help managers access information in a more understandable form. Finance professionals could then spend more time interpreting results, discussing options and supporting strategic decisions.

Critical evaluation and risks

Generative AI can produce inaccurate statements or calculations, so its output must not be treated as automatically correct. Entering confidential customer, employee or company financial information into an unapproved tool can also create privacy and security risks. Organisations should set clear rules about approved tools and permitted data, train employees, keep human review in the process and verify figures against trusted financial systems.

Visual artefact suggestion: Show a process diagram: approved financial data → AI-generated draft summary → finance professional checks figures and context → final management report.

Conclusion

Generative AI has potential to improve financial communication and productivity, but its use must be controlled through data protection, verification and clear accountability.
